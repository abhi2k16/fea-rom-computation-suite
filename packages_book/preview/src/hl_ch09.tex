\hlsection{The question}
\begin{hlquote}A damped cantilever will be part of a larger system, and all that system will ever ask of it is one thing: apply a force at the tip, read the tip displacement, at many frequencies. Here the reduction looks only at that input--output map.\end{hlquote}
\hlsection{Key concepts}
\begin{description}\setlength\itemsep{5pt}\setlength\parsep{0pt}\renewcommand{\descriptionlabel}[1]{\hspace{\labelsep}\sffamily\bfseries\color{hlnavy}#1}
\item[Transfer function, port] the input--output map that the reduced model must keep
\item[Balanced truncation] keeps states that are both easy to excite and easy to see; has an a priori bound
\item[Hankel singular values] rank the states by their effect on the input--output behaviour
\item[Krylov moment matching] exact response at an expansion point; accurate near it, not globally
\item[SOAR] moment matching in the second-order form, keeping a mechanical model
\item[Stability and passivity] properties a reduced model can lose, checked in the chapter
\end{description}
\hlsection{After this chapter you can}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{3pt}
\item choose among the three methods for a given port
\item check the bound and the stability of a reduced model
\item know that moment matching is accurate only near its expansion point
\end{itemize}
