\hlsection{The question}
\begin{hlquote}Sweep a damped beam over frequency and the response is flat for long stretches and then jumps at each resonance. A reduced model needs full-order snapshots, each an expensive solve, and the question is where to take them: evenly spaced, or where the response is hardest.\end{hlquote}
\vspace{8pt}\begin{hlsum}\textbf{Snapshots belong where the error is largest, and greedy training finds those places without being told.} With 5 basis columns the modal basis errs by \cfrModalNearFive{} near resonance and greedy training reaches \cfrGreedyNearFive; uniform training gives \cfrUniformNearFive, comparable. Greedy is not uniformly better: it gave a lower median error at \cfrGreedyWinsMed{} of \cfrRankCount{} sizes but a higher maximum at four of seven. The residual norm signals where to add the next snapshot, and the certified bound is rigorous but loose.\end{hlsum}
\hlsection{Key concepts}
\begin{description}\setlength\itemsep{5pt}\setlength\parsep{0pt}\renewcommand{\descriptionlabel}[1]{\hspace{\labelsep}\sffamily\bfseries\color{hlnavy}#1}
\item[Frequency-dependent operator] $\bm A(\omega)=-\omega^2\bm M+i\omega\bm C+\bm K$ is nearly singular at resonance
\item[Greedy training] add the next snapshot where the current model is judged worst
\item[Error indicator] ranks frequencies cheaply, but does not measure the error
\item[Certified bound] a number the true error cannot exceed, via a lower bound on the smallest singular value
\item[Random vibration] response to a power spectral density through the reduced model
\item[Break-even] offline cost against saving per query
\end{description}
