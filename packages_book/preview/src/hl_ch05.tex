\hlbanner{Part II $\cdot$ Full-order physics}{5}{Material and geometric nonlinearity}
\hlsection{The question}
\begin{hlquote}A shallow two-bar truss is pushed down at its apex. At first it resists, then at a certain load it gives way and snaps through, and a solver that steps up the load simply stops converging just above that load.\end{hlquote}
\vspace{8pt}\begin{hlsum}\textbf{Past the limit load there is no equilibrium state for load control to find, so displacement control or arc length must be used instead.} On the snap-through truss, load control fails at $1.1\,P_{max}$, while displacement control and arc length follow the whole curve to \cnlTrussErrDC{} and \cnlTrussErrAL{} against the closed form. The chapter explains the Newton iteration that all drivers share, verifies force and tangent by rotation invariance and finite differences, and adds small-strain plasticity checked against a hand solution.\end{hlsum}
\hlsection{Key concepts}
\begin{description}\setlength\itemsep{5pt}\setlength\parsep{0pt}\renewcommand{\descriptionlabel}[1]{\hspace{\labelsep}\sffamily\bfseries\color{hlnavy}#1}
\item[Newton iteration] residual and tangent stiffness, shared by every driver
\item[Total-Lagrangian kinematics] Green--Lagrange strain is unchanged by rigid rotation
\item[Tangent consistency] the tangent must be the derivative of the force, checked by finite differences
\item[Hyperelasticity, $J_2$ plasticity] stored-energy law; permanent strain with history variables
\item[Limit point] the load reaches a maximum and the tangent is singular
\item[Path following] load control, displacement control, arc length and Koiter--Newton
\end{description}
\hlsection{After this chapter you can}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{3pt}
\item state the Newton iteration the drivers share and verify force and tangent by rotation invariance and finite differences
\item choose among four path-following methods for a response with a limit point
\item check a plastic load cycle against a hand solution
\end{itemize}

\hlsection{Headline result}
\begin{hlheadline}Load control fails at $1.1\,P_{max}$, because no equilibrium state of that branch exists. Displacement control follows the whole curve to \cnlTrussErrDC{} (relative error against the closed form) and arc length to \cnlTrussErrAL.\end{hlheadline}

\hlsection{A stated limit}
\begin{hllimit}Plasticity is small-strain with isotropic linear hardening: there is no kinematic hardening, rate dependence or finite-strain plasticity.\end{hllimit}
